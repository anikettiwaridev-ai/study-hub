# Past papers

Six mid-semester papers and two end-semester papers, every question solved in the layout you would write in the exam. The [mock paper](../mock-mst) is the seventh mid-semester, written in the shape of the latest two.

::: tip Which papers count most
1. **[Oct 2025](./mst-2025-oct)**: last year's paper for this course. Do it first, timed.
2. **[2024 Paper B](./mst-2024-b)**: your exact course code (CSN3001 / AIN3001).
3. **[Mar 2024 Paper A](./mst-2024-mar)**: the sibling CSE-DS course, same syllabus.
4. **[Sep 2023](./mst-2023-sep)**, **[2022 Paper B](./mst-2022-b)** and **[Oct 2022 Paper A](./mst-2022-a)**: older papers for other branches, but most of their questions sit inside your syllabus.

:::

## How the mid-semester is set {#pattern}

Across the six papers (170 marks), these topics came back:

| Topic | Papers | Typical marks | Where to learn it |
|---|---|---|---|
| An expression question (infix → postfix or prefix, or evaluating postfix) | **6 of 6** | 5–6 | [Unit 3](../notes/unit-3) |
| A linked-list function to write, or a list trace | **6 of 6** | 5–12 | [Unit 2](../notes/unit-2) |
| A queue question: circular queue, deque or priority-queue trace, or a queue program | **6 of 6** | 5–6 | [Unit 4](../notes/unit-4) |
| A recurrence relation | **5 of 6** | 5–7 | [Unit 1](../notes/unit-1#substitution) |
| Biased Search | 2 of 6 (Sep 2023 and Oct 2025) | 5 | [Unit 1](../notes/unit-1#biased-search) |
| The recursive `next->next` list print | 2 of 6 (Oct 2022 and Sep 2023) | 3 | [Unit 2](../notes/unit-2#recursive-trace) |
| Complexity arguments (which structure, which worst case) | 3 of 6 | 2–6 | [Unit 2](../notes/unit-2#toolkit) |

**The latest two papers share a template**: one conversion, one recurrence, one queue question, one or two linked-list functions in C++, and one extra (Biased Search, or worst-case complexities). This year Unit 5 joins the syllabus, so expect a tree question too. [How often each topic repeats](../repeats) has the full map.

::: tip Attempting a paper
Hide the solutions (the default), set a 90-minute timer, and write on paper. Then open the solutions and mark yourself honestly. Tick **Mark done** only when you could produce the answer without looking.
:::
